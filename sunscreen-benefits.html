What does sunscreen do?
A clearer daily start.

Understand the benefits of sunscreen, what SPF50 versus SPF100 means and how to choose a formula that fits your day.

By Aureveil editorial · Reviewed 5 November 2026

Sunscreen benefitsSkin aging & wrinklesSPF50 vs SPF100Skin fitYour daily routine

What are the benefits
of sunscreen?

Sunscreen reduces the amount of ultraviolet radiation reaching your skin. Broad-spectrum protection covers UVA and UVB: both can damage skin. UVB is strongly associated with sunburn; UVA contributes to premature skin aging. Look beyond the SPF number to the product’s UVA protection too.

Correctly used sunscreen helps prevent sunburn and UV-related early signs of aging. It belongs alongside shade, protective clothing and a hat, whether your day includes a lunch break outdoors in Singapore or a longer afternoon in the sun.

Does sunscreen
prevent wrinkles?

Daily sunscreen helps limit UV-related premature skin aging, including some changes associated with wrinkles. Prevention is a consistent habit: texture and finish are immediate experiences, while changes in skin aging are studied over longer periods.

In a randomized trial of 903 Australian adults under 55, daily sunscreen users had 24% lower odds of increased skin aging over 4.5 years than discretionary users. Researchers assessed the back of the hand using another sunscreen formula. The figure describes the odds of aging progression in that study; it is not a percentage of facial wrinkles removed or an Aureveil test result.

SPF50 vs SPF100.
What changes?

SPF describes protection against sunburn-producing radiation, predominantly UVB, under standardized testing. A higher number provides more protection, with smaller differences in the percentage filtered as numbers rise.

Can SPF100 offer a real benefit?

Yes. A randomized, double-blind beach study of 55 people found less sunburn with the tested SPF100+ formula than the tested SPF50+ formula over five days. This supports the value of higher protection in that setting; the study used other formulas and assessed sunburn, not pore clogging.

Kohli et al., 2020: read the SPF100+ versus SPF50+ trial (opens in a new tab)

Is SPF50 enough for daily use?

AAD recommends SPF30 or higher, broad-spectrum protection and water resistance. SPF50 satisfies the SPF-number part of that guidance. Check the other label information and your plans too. For swimming or heavy sweating, select clearly labelled water resistance and follow its directions.

Does sunscreen clog pores?
Choose by the formula.

An SPF100 label does not tell you that a sunscreen will block pores. SPF measures sun protection, while pore compatibility depends on the finished formula and your skin. A lower number is not a promise of fewer breakouts.

For oily or acne-prone skin, AAD advises looking for a product labelled “non-comedogenic” or “won’t clog pores.” A light texture or a positive review alone does not establish that rating.

What should I check in sunscreen for sensitive skin?

Start with the full ingredient list and ingredients you already know you avoid. With eczema, rosacea, active irritation or prescribed treatment, ask your clinician about formula fit. Our skin suitability guide helps you identify the questions to raise.

Before sunscreen.
Your morning, in order.

Complete your usual skincare and moisturiser first, apply sunscreen, then add makeup once it settles. Follow the label, cover exposed skin generously and apply before going outdoors. Reapply at least every two hours outdoors and after swimming or sweating, following any water-resistance instructions.
